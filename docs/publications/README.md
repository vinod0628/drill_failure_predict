# Publications: two journal manuscripts and a PhD thesis

All three are based on the Drilling Failure Prediction & Prevention System (DFPS) and the **SB3 demonstration
workspace** (`demo_data/sb3_workspace`). The drilling histories are a **synthetic, process-based benchmark**
generated by `dfpredict/risk/demo.py`, and every manuscript says so. Field validation is stated as future work.

**Titles**
* Thesis: *Prediction and Prevention of Core-Drilling Failures under Geological-Model Uncertainty: Theory, System
  Development and Application to Mineral Exploration in Proterozoic–Palaeozoic Sedimentary Basins*.
* Paper 1 (method and validation): *Transferable ahead-of-the-bit prediction of core-drilling failures using a
  discrete-time hazard model, rock-mechanical descriptors and explainable gradient boosting*.
* Paper 2 (decision support): *Drillhole Planning and Evidence-Ranked Countermeasures under Geological-Model
  Uncertainty: An Uncertainty-Aware Decision-Support Workflow Demonstrated on a Karstified, Faulted Sedimentary
  Benchmark*.

The two papers do not overlap. Paper 1 covers the hazard models, leave-one-area-out validation, ablations and SHAP.
Paper 2 covers ongoing-hole forecasting, contact ties, rule fusion, countermeasure ranking and plan checks, and cites
Paper 1 as a companion paper (in preparation).

## Results worth knowing before review
* On this benchmark, removing the rock-mechanical descriptors does not measurably change performance. Over three
  seeds, instability recall is 0.773 ± 0.023 with them and 0.754 ± 0.029 without, because the coarse lithology
  classes of the generator carry most of the signal. The manuscripts report this openly.
* The strongest methodological result is that random hole-level splits are optimistic compared with
  leave-one-area-out (C 0.787 vs 0.754, trajectory AUC 0.718 vs 0.563).
* Trajectory and bit failures are too rare in the benchmark for useful models.
